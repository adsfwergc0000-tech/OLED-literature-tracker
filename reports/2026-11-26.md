### 1. [Transport Layer-Driven Optimization of Color and Efficiency in TADF OLEDs for Large-Area Applications](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33933v1)

**Authors:** Manish Kumar, Jawid Nikan, Luiz Pereira  
**Venue/category:** physics.optics  
**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**arXiv:** `2611.33933`  
**Source(s):** arXiv  
**Keyword score:** 22  
**Matched:** thermally activated delayed fluorescence, tadf, oled, organic light-emitting diode, emissive layer  

**Abstract**

Balancing structural simplicity with high efficiency is crucial for practical applications of organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs), especially in large-area lighting devices. Optimizing the charge-transport layers in multilayer OLEDs controls the recombination zone within the emissive layer, thereby enhancing the device's performance. Herein, we experimentally and computationally investigate how the thicknesses of the electron and hole transport layers affect the performance of orange-red thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) OLEDs. Using a TADF emitter, 2-[4-(diphenylamino)phenyl]-10,10-dioxide-9H-thioxanthen-9-one (TXO-TPA), at 10 wt\% concentration in 4,4-Bis(N-carbazolyl)-1,1-biphenyl (CBP) host, we achieve a maximum external quantum efficiency ($η_{EQE}$) of 20.86\% and a peak luminance of 32,800 cd m$^{-2}$. Using a combination of drift-diffusion and optical simulations, we reveal how variations in hole- and electron-transport-layer thicknesses modulate the charge-carrier balance and shift the recombination zone within the emissive layer. This tuning not only optimizes exciton formation but also enables color tuning from green to red without altering the emissive material. Our findings highlight the critical role of transport layer engineering in achieving efficient, color-controlled OLEDs with simplified architectures. Furthermore, we successfully demonstrate a large-area OLED device (~20 cm$^{2}$), achieving an impressive $η_{EQE}$ of 17\%, underscoring the scalability and practicality of our approach for general lighting applications.
